"The World in One Page" — all of Tuesday 24 November 2026 on a single square broadsheet. Fifty stories, every one of them first reported that Tuesday, are numbered 1 to 50 in one unbroken sequence: sixteen are pictured in hand-drawn panels (four large leads and twelve minis, each carrying a confidence chip and its source), and the other thirty-four run as briefs set in a large, styled serif with bold navy numbers and small-capital source tags, so even the small print reads comfortably. Under the masthead a UTC timeline walks through the day as it happened; region and hard-number strips tally it up; and an itemised date check names the near-miss stories that were left out because they broke on other days. Nothing on the page is a photograph — every pictured story is drawn.

## The map

The bottom-left panel holds an atlas-style world map with real continent silhouettes. Its markers carry no numbers: the sixteen pictured stories are warm gold dots at the places they happened — the New Zealand stories gather into one tight cluster on the islands — and the briefs are smaller, paler dots. The caption under the panel heading says exactly that, and a "HOW SURE" legend beneath the map explains the confidence chips used across the page.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

That publishes the stored artwork to `/output/entry.png`. The image is staged in a scratch file, flushed to disk, verified against the SHA-256 digest recorded in `main.py`, and only then renamed into place — so the picture that gets judged is provably the picture stored in this repository.
